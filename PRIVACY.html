PULSE privacy policy

Effective from PULSE 1.0.0.

PULSE shares the audio of a browser tab you choose into an X Space you are speaking in. It collects no personal data, and it has no servers.

What PULSE reads. On x.com and twitter.com PULSE runs inside the page, to find the Space's audio connection and to read whether you are on stage and whether X has your microphone muted. It does not read your posts, messages, account details or browsing.

Audio. The tab you pick in Chrome's own share dialog is sent over the connection X opened for your Space. Your microphone stays out of it unless you press the Voice key, and then it is mixed in, in your browser. PULSE sends them nowhere else, records nothing and keeps nothing. Once in a Space, people listening hear what you share, as they hear your voice; X's own terms and policies apply to that.

The tab you share. If you use the player keys or M, Chrome asks you once to let PULSE reach your tabs. With that, PULSE plays, pauses, moves, mutes and sets the volume of the audio or video player in the tab you share, and reads its position and length. It looks at which tabs are making sound to find that tab, and marks them for a moment when you start a Share, so that Chrome's capture can say which one you chose. It reads nothing else from them, and sends nothing anywhere. Say no and PULSE simply cannot control the player. You can remove the access in Chrome at any time.

What PULSE keeps. Your tab level, your Width setting, and where you left the panel, in your browser's extension storage on this device. Uninstalling PULSE removes them.

What PULSE sends. Nothing to the developer or to any third party. No analytics, no advertising, no remote code.

Your choices. Chrome asks you to pick a tab each time, and shows that the tab is being shared. Press Stop, or close the tab, and X has your microphone again as if PULSE were not there.

PULSE is not affiliated with or endorsed by X Corp.

Questions: contact the developer through the extension's Chrome Web Store page.
